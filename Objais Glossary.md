# Objais Glossary

Shared terms for the revenue and acquisition guides, covering the current business and future options. Open a definition when you need it; the guides explain their examples without requiring a glossary read-through.

Start with [the revenue guide](<Objais Revenue Model Numerical Summary.md>) or [the acquisition guide](<Objais Acquisition Numerical Summary.md>).

Terms describe how these documents count people, tasks and money. Their source links lead to the underlying model or research. They do not establish that Objais has a particular contract, capability or measured result.

## Distinctions that change the numbers

- **Owner, interaction and opportunity:** one person can click several times, own several items and make several requests. Each needs its own counting unit.
- **Paid event and payable event:** these are two names for the same contractual earning condition here. A product event or card sale is not automatically one.
- **CAC, CPA and CPC:** owner CAC counts distinct new owners and full acquisition costs. CPA names another outcome and its cost scope; CPC counts advertising clicks.
- **Conversion rates:** the owner model’s q, the share of requests accepted and the share of clicks producing a fee begin with different groups. Always keep the denominator.
- **Fee, premium and cash:** the insurance premium is not Objais’s fee. Earning the fee, invoicing it and receiving the money are stages of the same amount.
- **Retention and renewal:** app use, opportunity continuation, cover and software payments follow different clocks. A return earns another fee only when the agreement permits it.

## People and routes

### Acquisition

Bringing a new owner into the measured service. [Source](<research/revenue-model/README.md>)

### Actor

A person or organisation participating in the service, such as an owner, representative, Objais or an insurer. Roles explain who acts and who pays; they are not interchangeable counting units. Also used: Actors. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model Diagrams.html>)

### Adoption

The share of prospects who join the measured service. [Source](<research/revenue-model/README.md>)

### Advertising

Paid messages or placements that bring people toward the service. Compare their downstream outcomes as well as their reach. [Source](<Objais Funnels Advertising Metrics and UX Research.md#advertising-platforms-and-distribution-choices>)

### Advertising inventory

The ad space a publisher or network makes available to buy. A large or relevant audience does not establish that this space is offered to outside advertisers. [Source](<Objais External Advertising Inventory Research.md>)

### Advertising network

A service connecting advertisers with space across participating publishers’ websites or apps. This is a media-buying route, distinct from a community platform such as WhatsApp. [Source](<Objais External Advertising Inventory Research.md>)

### Asset owner

One person whose possessions are being considered. Three watches and a car still count as one owner; a representative acting for them is not an extra owner. Also used: Owner. [Source](<research/revenue-model/README.md>)

### Channel

The route that brings someone in, such as an advertisement or a dealer introduction. [Source](<research/revenue-model/README.md>)

### Community

A particular group using a network. Membership does not establish that someone saw an invitation or became an Objais owner. [Source](<Objais Networks and Communities Research.md#separate-the-network-from-the-community-and-its-organiser>)

### Customer

In the consumer revenue examples, the asset owner using Objais, including someone using it for free. The insurer or broker paying a lead fee has a different role; a future professional offer would need its own account unit. [Source](<research/revenue-model/README.md>)

### Network

The underlying platform, such as WhatsApp or Facebook. One network can host many separate communities. This community meaning is different from an advertising network that buys media across publishers. [Source](<Objais Networks and Communities Research.md#separate-the-network-from-the-community-and-its-organiser>)

### Operator

The person or business organising a community or its activity, such as an administrator, store or auction host. An operator may run several groups and is a different role from the owner of a collectible. [Source](<Objais Networks and Communities Research.md#separate-the-network-from-the-community-and-its-organiser>)

### Placement

The particular banner, feed item, app surface or sponsored message where an advertisement appears. Availability depends on the publisher, country, format and offer. [Source](<Objais External Advertising Inventory Research.md>)

### Prospect

A distinct person reached before they join the service. [Source](<research/revenue-model/README.md>)

### Representative

Someone authorised to act for an owner, such as a staff member or adviser. [Source](<research/revenue-model/README.md>)

### Acquisition cost (CAC)

Total attributable cost of bringing in new owners, divided by the number of distinct newly acquired owners. Include advertising, introduction fees and onboarding work; clicks, installs and returning owners do not automatically add to that denominator. Also used: Owner CAC. [Source](<research/revenue-model/README.md>)

## Tasks and payment conditions

### Activation

The first useful product result under a stated rule, such as obtaining a usable estimate in the proposed photo journey. This product milestone is separate from activation of insurance cover. [Source](<Objais Funnels Advertising Metrics and UX Research.md#the-customer-journey-and-commercial-funnel>)

### Commerce event

A listing, auction, sale, exchange, shipment or collection update that creates a possible task need. It does not automatically earn Objais a fee. [Source](<Objais Networks and Communities Research.md#separate-the-network-from-the-community-and-its-organiser>)

### Commission

The agreed payment for arranging the cover. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Eligible owner

An owner with an opportunity that can still earn its first fee. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Funnel

A sequence connecting arrival, a useful task, a request and a commercial outcome. Different entry routes may have different steps while reaching comparable payment conditions. [Source](<Objais Funnels Advertising Metrics and UX Research.md#the-customer-journey-and-commercial-funnel>)

### Insurance policy

The contract providing insurance cover. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Intent

Evidence that someone wants the offered help, such as a request for a personalised quotation. Interest in trying a feature alone does not establish insurance intent. [Source](<Objais Funnels Advertising Metrics and UX Research.md#the-customer-journey-and-commercial-funnel>)

### Lead

An insurance enquiry passed to a partner. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Opportunity

A possible transaction that could earn Objais a fee. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Paid event

An occurrence that meets the agreement’s condition for earning an Objais fee. Both guides also call this a payable event. The fee may be earned before cash arrives, and the contract determines whether acceptance, policy binding or another occurrence qualifies. Also used: Payable event. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Paid lead

A lead accepted as payable under the partner agreement. “Paid” describes the entitlement to the fee in these examples; it does not establish that money has already arrived. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Policy binding

The point when insurance cover becomes effective under its contract. A quotation or accepted enquiry does not by itself establish effective cover. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Premium

The price of the insurance. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Qualification

Checking whether a request meets the relevant country, asset and service requirements. The criteria must be stated; a qualified request is not automatically payable. [Source](<Objais Funnels Advertising Metrics and UX Research.md#the-customer-journey-and-commercial-funnel>)

### Qualified request

An enquiry that meets the agreed requirements. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Referral fee

An agreed payment to Objais when a service introduction meets its payment condition. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Trading card game (TCG)

A game using collectible trading cards. In these guides, TCG identifies a possible collection and community market; it does not establish Objais recognition capability, insurance availability or paid demand. Also used: TCG. [Source](<Objais Networks and Communities Research.md#match-the-offer-to-the-moment>)

### Trading cards

Collectible cards used in games or collected for subjects such as sports. Trading card games (TCGs) are one part of this market; sports cards need their own demand and service assumptions. [Source](<Collectibles Market Research.md#trading-card-games-and-sports-cards>)

### Conversion probability

The monthly chance of an active eligible owner reaching the initial payable event: q = n × p × α. Payment-history rules and delivery capacity may reduce realised events further. Click-to-request and request-to-payment yields have different denominators. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Need probability

The chance that an active eligible owner needs help in the month being modelled. The symbol is n; need can depend on season or time since acquisition. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Paid acceptance

The chance that a qualified request meets the contractual payment condition. The symbol is α (alpha). Under a binding arrangement, the condition is effective cover rather than lead acceptance. Also used: Alpha. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Request probability

The chance of a qualified request, given that an active eligible owner needs help. The symbol is p. This is a conditional rate, not a share of all visitors. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

## Costs and money

### Cash receipts

Money actually received. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Fixed costs

Expenses assumed to stay the same within the volume being considered, such as the monthly overhead used later in this guide. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Funding gap

The cash shortfall that needs financing when expenses fall due before enough receipts arrive. The largest shortfall can occur before the end of the forecast, even if the final cash balance is positive. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Invoice

A request for payment. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Payment reversal

A fee withdrawn or credited back. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Receivable

An amount earned and still awaiting payment. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Revenue

The amount Objais has earned. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Variable costs

Costs that change with the people served or work done, such as service and processing expenses. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Break-even

The point where the stated result reaches zero. Owner contribution, company operating result and cash result have different break-even conditions; always name the result and period. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Cash result

Money received minus expenses paid during that period. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Company operating result

Revenue less variable, acquisition and fixed costs during the calendar period being considered. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Competition multiplier

An assumed or measured factor that changes prospect costs over time to represent cost pressure. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Contribution

Revenue minus variable costs and acquisition costs. Fixed costs have not yet been deducted. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Cost curve

The relationship between the number of prospects reached and the cost of reaching them. In this model, a rising curve makes additional prospects more expensive. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Cost per acquisition (CPA)

Cost divided by the number of a named outcome, such as submitted requests or payable events. State the cost scope: a media-only CPA excludes introduction and onboarding costs. Owner CAC uses full attributable costs and distinct new owners. Also used: CPA. [Source](<Objais Funnels Advertising Metrics and UX Research.md#translate-the-funnel-into-acquisition-limits>)

### Cost per click (CPC)

Media spend divided by outbound clicks in the stated campaign and period. It measures advertising cost per click, not the full cost per acquired owner. Also used: CPC, Media CPC. [Source](<Objais Metrics Research.md#media-cpc>)

### Cost per thousand impressions (CPM)

Media spend divided by impressions, multiplied by 1,000. Keep the platform, reporting period and currency consistent. Also used: CPM, Media CPM. [Source](<Objais Metrics Research.md#media-cpm>)

### Percentage point

The difference between two percentage rates. Moving from 95% to 96% is one percentage point; it is not a 1% relative increase. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Return on advertising spend (ROAS)

Attributable revenue divided by advertising spend. These examples use Objais fees after expected reversals, not premiums or asset values. A ratio above one can still leave a loss after delivery costs. Also used: ROAS, Objais revenue ROAS. [Source](<Objais Metrics Research.md#objais-revenue-roas>)

## Returning, renewing and paying

### Cohort

A group followed from a stated entry period or experiment. The revenue guide groups distinct owners by acquisition month; the acquisition guide can follow linked entry journeys. State the counting unit and entry rule before comparing cohorts. Also used: Owner cohort, Entry cohort. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Paid account

The record of that billing relationship. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Paid software

A software service sold for a recurring payment. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Renewal

Continuing insurance cover for another term. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Renewal payment rights

The contractual right for Objais to receive a payment when a policy renews. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Retention

Continuation in a specified relationship over time. App use, an active opportunity, insurance cover and software billing each need their own measure; continuation in one does not prove continuation in another. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Billed continuation

The share of paid accounts still paying next month. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Billing churn

The share of paid accounts whose payments stop. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Cooldown

A minimum gap between initial paid events, such as six months. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Customer contribution over a stated horizon

Discounted revenue less variable and acquisition costs, per originally acquired owner over the stated period. The guide normally uses 36 months and a 1% monthly discount rate. Fixed costs are excluded. Also used: 36-month customer contribution, Lifetime contribution. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Discounting

Giving money earned later less weight than money earned now when comparing amounts across time. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Dormant states (D_E and D_M)

Owners who have gone quiet but may return, retaining the unpaid or paid status of their opportunity. Return preserves owner identity and payment history. Also used: D_E, D_M, Dormancy. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Eligible state (E)

An active owner whose current opportunity has not yet earned its initial payment. Payment history and capacity still determine whether an event can occur this month. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Monetised state (M)

An active owner whose current opportunity has earned its initial payment. Continued use alone cannot earn that same fee again. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Monthly continuation rate

The share remaining in the opportunity relationship next month. It can differ before and after payment. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Net monthly payment

What Objais retains after payment fees or partner shares, before service costs. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Owner-month

One owner served for one month. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Permanent exit (X)

The opportunity relationship has ended in the model. This does not automatically end a policy or software billing relationship. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Policy survival

The share of policies still in force each month. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Reset rate

The share of continuing monetised owners who gain a genuinely new eligible opportunity. The symbol is λ (lambda). Another item or return visit qualifies only when the agreement supports a new fee. Also used: Lambda. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

### Rolling payment cap

A limit on initial paid events for each owner over every consecutive window, such as two events in any 12 months. [Source](<Objais Revenue Model.tex>)

## Measurement and experience

### Attribution

Assigning credit for an outcome to a channel or interaction under a stated rule. Several platforms can claim the same outcome; their claims do not create several underlying fees. [Source](<Objais Funnels Advertising Metrics and UX Research.md#feedback-to-advertising-systems>)

### Comprehension

Whether a person understands the result, checked against a stated question or criterion. In the proposed research, owners explain the estimate in their own words. [Source](<Objais Funnels Advertising Metrics and UX Research.md#proposed-first-usability-round>)

### Event

A recorded occurrence, such as a request submission or fee being earned. A product event or commerce event is not necessarily a payable event. [Source](<Objais Funnels Advertising Metrics and UX Research.md#proposed-event-sequence>)

### Holdout

A comparison group not exposed to the intervention being studied. A credible comparison can help estimate its additional effect; a small pilot may support only a directional conclusion. [Source](<Objais Funnels Advertising Metrics and UX Research.md#feedback-to-advertising-systems>)

### Impression

One appearance of an advertisement in platform reporting. Several impressions can belong to the same person. [Source](<research/acquisition/metrics.csv>)

### Incrementality

The additional activity caused by an intervention, such as an advertisement. Assigning channel credit alone cannot establish this effect. [Source](<Objais Funnels Advertising Metrics and UX Research.md#feedback-to-advertising-systems>)

### Latency

Elapsed time between a defined start and result. State the attempts included; report failures separately rather than making them disappear from performance results. [Source](<Objais Funnels Advertising Metrics and UX Research.md#diagnostic-metrics>)

### Mature cohort

A cohort that has had the agreed observation window. Maturity does not make every request resolved: show pending outcomes and their ages separately. [Source](<Objais Funnels Advertising Metrics and UX Research.md#decision-rules>)

### Metric

A measure used to understand an outcome or diagnose a stage of the journey. Each metric needs a counting unit, denominator, period and source. Also used: Metrics. [Source](<Objais Funnels Advertising Metrics and UX Research.md#metrics-to-track>)

### Reconciliation

Checking that product, partner and finance records agree about the same underlying opportunities and fees. It helps expose retries, duplicates and mismatched decisions. [Source](<Objais Funnels Advertising Metrics and UX Research.md#measurement-architecture-and-attribution>)

### Segmentation

Separating results by meaningful differences, such as country, asset, channel, partner, device or acquisition period. Small groups may still be too uncertain to compare reliably. [Source](<Objais Funnels Advertising Metrics and UX Research.md#metrics-to-track>)

### Task success

Whether the intended task is completed correctly under a stated rule. Finishing screens quickly does not establish that the result is understood. [Source](<Objais Funnels Advertising Metrics and UX Research.md#proposed-first-usability-round>)

### Time to value

Time from a defined start to the first useful result. For the proposed photo journey, that result could be a usable estimate; it is not the time until insurance cover begins. [Source](<Objais Funnels Advertising Metrics and UX Research.md#diagnostic-metrics>)

### User experience (UX)

What it takes for a person to complete their task and understand the result: effort, errors, recovery and confidence, including any partner handoff. Also used: User experience. [Source](<Objais Funnels Advertising Metrics and UX Research.md#user-experience-research>)

### 95th percentile (p95)

A value at or below which 95% of the measured observations fall. For completed-attempt latency, it describes the slower tail; it does not account for excluded failures. Also used: p95. [Source](<Objais Funnels Advertising Metrics and UX Research.md#diagnostic-metrics>)

### Click-through rate (CTR)

Outbound link clicks divided by impressions for the same platform and period. The example’s 1,000 clicks from 20,000 impressions give 5%; other definitions of “click” would change the measure. Also used: CTR, Outbound CTR. [Source](<Objais Metrics Research.md#outbound-ctr>)

### Median (p50)

The middle value of ordered observations, also called the 50th percentile. For completed-attempt times, it describes a typical completion; failed attempts still need their own count. Also used: Median, p50, 50th percentile. [Source](<Objais Funnels Advertising Metrics and UX Research.md#diagnostic-metrics>)

### Payable yield

The share reaching a payable event from a named starting group. Twenty-five payable events mean 50% of 50 submissions, 62.5% of 40 qualified requests, or 2.5% of 1,000 entry clicks; these rates cannot be substituted for one another. [Source](<Objais Metrics Research.md#payable-yield>)

## Evidence status

### Assumed

Chosen to explore a possibility. [Source](<Objais Revenue Research Strategy.md>)

### Documented

Supported by an agreement or other written evidence. [Source](<Objais Revenue Research Strategy.md>)

### Measured

Based on actual records. [Source](<Objais Revenue Research Strategy.md>)

### Unknown

The evidence is still missing. [Source](<Objais Revenue Research Strategy.md>)

## What still needs confirmation

The vocabulary is consistent across the guides. The team still needs to supply the contractual payment conditions, observed behaviour, full costs and collection dates. Future paid demand, professional account units and availability in each country remain open commercial questions.

The [metric dictionary](<Objais Metrics Research.md#full-metric-dictionary>) retains the detailed formulas, counting rules and observation windows. The [event dictionary](<Objais Measurement and Attribution Research.md#full-proposed-event-dictionary>) retains the proposed occurrence and identity rules. This glossary explains their terms; the dictionaries and model hold the operational detail.
