# Objais Research Strategy for a Revenue Model

**13 October 2026 | Global evidence with Brazil and Europe highlighted**

The objective is to build a revenue model that explains **who pays Objais, which event earns the payment, how often that event repeats, and what it costs to deliver**. The model should show whether the current lead-sale proposition can earn an acceptable contribution, whether continuing services improve it, and whether entering collectibles offers a better commercial opportunity than improving existing markets.

Begin with commercial terms and the actual funnel. Existing market research already supplies a useful demand and competitor baseline; another large market-size estimate will not reveal Objais’s net fee, accepted-lead yield or renewal rights. The first research priority is the evidence that could change the business decision.

The planned output is a **36-month monthly cohort model**, with revenue, contribution, operating costs and cash timing by segment, country and acquisition channel. Native EUR and BRL inputs remain separate; a dated exchange-rate convention enables an explicitly labelled consolidated view. Every important input has a definition, source, status and uncertainty range.

This strategy designs the research and model work. Interviews, commercial experiments and the ten-stage route have not been executed. The research workbook provides the requests, parameters, gates and validation requirements needed to start.

# Decisions the research must answer

The model should support five concrete decisions:

1. Whether accepted lead sales can produce positive contribution after acquisition and fulfilment costs.
2. Whether policy commissions, renewals, service referrals or paid collection tools are available and improve the result.
3. Which segment-country-channel combination deserves the first bounded commercial test.
4. Whether recurring collection activity creates paid value sufficient to cover its additional cost.
5. Whether the next investment should improve existing categories, enter a collectible category, change distribution, or defer the offer.

A profitable insurer relationship and frequent consumer app use are different outcomes. Either may matter, but the model must connect each to actual payment rights and costs. It should allow an evidence-based decision to revise or stop a hypothesis rather than force a preferred expansion.

# Start from the existing evidence

Use the [company research](Objais Research Report.md), [current markets research](Objais Current Markets Research.md) and [collectibles research](Collectibles Market Research.md) as the baseline. Their quantitative registers contain 220 observations and 96 source entries across 93 distinct URLs. Those counts describe the stored evidence, not independent studies or financial inputs.

The source registers use overlapping identifiers, so imports must preserve separate namespaces for current markets and collectibles. Retain each record’s period, currency, geography, qualifier and underlying study. A second page discussing the same report does not become independent confirmation.

The working commercial assumption is the team’s user-reported description: selling leads without demonstrated retention. The public FAQ describes commissions and service referrals. These may refer to different payment arrangements; neither resolves the contract. Stage 2 must reconcile them before assigning revenue to a supported base case.

Classify each category and country as advertised, actually transacted, planned or unresolved. Research Portugal and Brazil as individual operating cases, with other European markets added only when a specific country and counterparty are identified. Global and UK examples can supply comparators without becoming local placement rights.

# Research route

The route is sequential because later work depends on consistent commercial definitions and denominators. A single research lead maintains the evidence chain; subject-matter and finance owners supply or review their evidence. No subagent delegation is required.

| Stage | Research task | Required output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Freeze scope and reconcile existing evidence | Scope, source register and unresolved assumptions |
| 2 | Establish payments and commercial rights | Payable events, fees, splits, reversals and future rights |
| 3 | Define and reconcile funnel events and costs | Metric dictionary, observed cohorts and cost register |
| 4 | Select at most two research lanes | Segment-country-channel choices with reasons |
| 5 | Research paid demand, repeat tasks and distribution | Task evidence and bounded experiment designs |
| 6 | Verify underwriting, data and service feasibility | Accepted product scope and source-linked model inputs |
| 7 | Build monthly revenue and cash scenarios | Cohort workbook, specifications and scenario outputs |
| 8 | Validate balances, formulas and uncertainty | Validation results, corrections and remaining gaps |
| 9 | Choose an architecture and next experiment | Proceed, revise, defer or stop decision |
| 10 | Deliver a reproducible model handoff | Workbook, evidence, decision memo and next bounded task |

The machine-readable dispatch names installed capabilities, input and output references, owners, gates, stop conditions and trace events. It is a design artifact. Structural validation does not establish that research has run, that commercial inputs are true or that forecast outputs are reliable.

# Evidence to obtain first

## Commercial evidence

The first request to the team should be compact enough to answer with contracts, written terms and matching finance records. Obtain:

- The payer and payable trigger for each existing category and country.
- The net amount or commission basis, partner split, conditions, refunds and clawbacks.
- Whether lead fees and binding commissions are alternatives or genuinely additional payments.
- Renewal, endorsement, servicing and referral rights, including who owns the continuing relationship.
- Invoice triggers, payment delay, unpaid balances and territory restrictions.

A policy premium is not Objais revenue. An accepted lead may earn a fee even when no policy binds, while a commission model may earn nothing until a policy is issued. Record the actual trigger. If a combined arrangement exists, document the incremental payment and prevent duplicate counting.

If private terms are unavailable, complete the model structure and labelled scenarios. Keep contractual inputs unknown and defer a calibrated forecast; do not invent a typical industry fee to fill the gap.

## Funnel and cost evidence

Request all available monthly history, preferably six to twelve months if the company has it. A shorter history is usable with a correspondingly limited claim. Link deidentified operational and finance records by category, country, channel and original cohort.

Define the observable acquisition unit. The initial estimate requires no account, so an anonymous estimate session cannot automatically be treated as a unique customer. Use a prospect or lead identity when actually available and keep uncertain links unresolved.

Follow requests through qualification, delivery, acceptance, quotation, policy binding, cancellation and payment. Separate duplicates, rejected requests, unpaid acceptances and invoice reversals. Retain the reason and timing of each outcome.

Costs must include acquisition, partner payouts, image processing, valuation data, storage, support, specialist time, payment processing and policy servicing where applicable. Attribute costs to their drivers. Failed requests and free returning users still consume resources. Keep fixed staff and overhead outside contribution before calculating operating results.

## Responsibilities

The commercial owner supplies payment and distribution terms; product and data owners supply event definitions and exports; operations supplies rejection, service and capacity evidence; finance reconciles invoices, receipts and cost conventions. The research lead maintains provenance and the operator owns commercial choices.

The workbook contains **22 prioritised data requests**. P0 items establish whether the baseline can be calibrated. P1 items examine repeat value and distribution. P2 items strengthen capacity, concentration, software and reporting forecasts.

# Compare revenue architectures

Keep the actual lead model as the baseline. Evaluate alternatives only where their rights, demand and delivery requirements are explicit.

| Architecture | Payable event | Evidence required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One-off lead sale | A qualified lead is accepted under the payer’s contract | Net fee, acceptance, duplicate/reversal rules and cash timing |
| Insurance distribution commission | A policy binds or another documented commission event occurs | Premium basis, rate, partner split, cancellations and payment rights |
| Renewal or ongoing insurance remuneration | An eligible policy renews or earns a documented service fee | Contractual rights, matured policy cohorts and servicing cost |
| Specialist service referral | A referred service is completed and becomes payable | Completion, fee, reversal and attribution rules |
| Paid collection software or documentation | An owner or business purchases a defined recurring service or completed task | Paid demand, price, billing unit, retention and delivery cost |

Do not assume that a lead-selling contract grants renewal income. An owner renewing through another intermediary may create no additional Objais revenue. Conversely, low app frequency does not eliminate a valuable recurring commission if Objais actually earns it.

Paid records or software are separate hypotheses. A free collection tracker, favourable interview comment or competitor subscription price cannot establish conversion to an Objais paid product. A business buyer may pay per account, seat, task or portfolio; define one unit before modelling it.

A partner-distributed offer is a channel choice, not automatically a new revenue stream. Its economic effect may be lower acquisition cost, higher acceptance, a different revenue split or better repeat access. Model those changes explicitly.

# Choose where to collect deeper evidence

Screen the advertised current categories and the proposed collectibles markets using existing evidence and actual access. Start with three candidate questions:

- **Watches and jewellery:** can accepted records and coverage updates create repeat commercial value within the existing offer?
- **TCGs:** can frequent collection changes produce paid demand or materially better accepted-lead economics?
- **Pets:** is there a useful care or documentation task that Objais can deliver alongside established insurer servicing?

Art, handbags, yachts, supercars, everyday cars, leisure boats and coins remain relevant desk comparators. They should enter deeper fieldwork if commercial evidence makes them more promising. The strategy does not predetermine a winning category.

Choose at most two segment-country-channel lanes for the first fieldwork cycle. Underwriting acceptance, a usable distribution relationship and deliverable service are prerequisites for a supported insurance lane. Then compare potential contribution, cost of obtaining evidence, service burden and confidence in the available inputs.

A watch enquiry through a Portuguese dealer and a TCG owner acquired through Brazilian paid search are different lanes. Avoid averaging their acquisition cost, acceptance or remuneration into a single customer model. Do not force a Portugal or Brazil sales forecast when local placement remains unsupported.

# Research paid demand and repeat behaviour

## Task discovery

A proposed initial qualitative sample is 24 adult owners, split approximately 12 per selected lane, plus six relevant commercial or service counterparties. Adjust the mix to the selected lanes and the remaining questions. This sample is directional and cannot estimate population conversion or retention accurately.

Ask participants to show a recent completed task: adding or selling an item, preparing documentation, arranging protection, seeking a valuation or using a covered benefit. Record who performed it, the current alternative, time or money spent, the outcome and the next likely occurrence. Distinguish owners who act directly from those who use delegates.

Look for a specific gap that Objais could complete reliably. Existing free tools and insurer apps are part of the comparison. A broad request for “better collection management” is insufficient to justify a paid offer.

## Price and distribution evidence

For a candidate task, specify the deliverable, proposed price, eligibility and service terms. Record actual payment, conditional commitment and stated interest as different evidence types. Offer prices remain hypotheses until supported by paid outcomes or credible terms.

A channel test should track eligible acquisitions through qualified requests to paid acceptance or binding, using the contract’s trigger. Measure channel cost, partner split, completion, rejection and delay. A platform’s audience total cannot establish an affordable acquisition route.

Quantitative samples should reflect the precision needed for the decision. For illustration, estimating a 10% independent conversion proportion to roughly plus or minus three percentage points at 95% confidence requires about 385 observations under a simple normal approximation. This is sampling arithmetic, not an assumed Objais conversion rate or a universal pilot size. Use an appropriate interval for the actual sample and account for clustered partner traffic.

## Repeat value

A separately commissioned 60–90 day pilot should record meaningful D30, D60 and D90 actions among users eligible to reach each window. Useful actions might be an accepted record update, a completed service request or a successful protection change. Notification opens should have their own metric.

For each return action, record any resulting payment and incremental service cost. Keep users who return but never need protection in the denominator. Their activity may be useful product evidence while producing no additional revenue.

A 90-day pilot cannot establish annual policy renewal or annual paid-software retention. Model those with explicit uncertainty until mature cohorts or appropriate contractual history are available. Interviews, pilots and priced offers remain proposed work; this strategy does not recruit users, submit quotes or charge customers.

# Verify fulfilment before forecasting growth

For selected insurance lanes, obtain category-country acceptance criteria, current products, policy and item limits, minimum premiums, valuation basis, ownership evidence, location and transit treatment. Identify who reviews the request and which facts cause rejection or repricing.

Verify the right and practical ability to update policies or support claims. An edited dashboard value does not amend cover. A useful recurring interface requires accepted records and a defined service relationship.

Verify reference-data coverage, licence terms and costs for the selected asset categories. Record scarce comparables, variant errors, authenticity boundaries and local valuation differences. A public API listing or a competitor’s feature does not establish production access for Objais.

Measure time, specialist involvement and support costs for successful and failed cases. Add partner capacity so the forecast cannot grow beyond assumed or evidenced delivery. A larger insured value can bring greater expert work and a longer payment cycle.

# Revenue model design

## Grain and evidence status

The primary model grain is **month × acquisition cohort × segment × country × channel × revenue architecture**. Policy renewal and paid-software cohorts retain their own start dates and eligibility rules.

Every parameter should include its name, definition, unit, denominator, period, native currency, source reference, owner, low/base/high values and evidence status. Use the statuses observed, contract-supported, proxy, scenario assumption, unavailable and not applicable. Do not turn an unknown into zero. Use zero when absence is established or a specific scenario explicitly disables the stream.

The workbook provides **34 parameter records**, with numeric inputs left blank. Existing public data support demand context and comparators, rather than filling private fees, paid conversion or retention.

## Monthly revenue formulas

Use observed or projected payable events in the period where the agreed management-reporting convention earns the amount:

- **Lead revenue:** accepted payable leads × net fee, less actual or explicitly modelled reversals.
- **New-policy commission:** eligible bound premium × contractual commission rate × Objais retained share, less clawbacks.
- **Renewal revenue:** eligible renewed policy cohort × the contractual net fee or commission basis.
- **Service referrals:** completed payable referrals × net fee, less reversals.
- **Paid software:** billable accounts, seats or tasks × the relevant price and service period.

Choose one commission basis and avoid subtracting the partner split twice from an already-net fee. If the same event appears in multiple streams, a documented contract must explain why both payments are earned.

For software, calculate active paying accounts from beginning accounts, churn and new paid additions. Do not apply annual churn as a monthly percentage or use product visits as paid retention. Distinguish an annual upfront receipt from revenue allocated over its service period under the finance owner’s management convention.

Track invoices and cash separately from revenue. Settlement delay, bad debt, refunds and working capital can make an apparently attractive contribution consume cash. Keep actual cash history available for reconciliation.

## Contribution and operating results

Subtract variable acquisition and delivery costs from net revenue to obtain contribution. Then subtract fixed staff, tools and overhead to obtain the modelled operating result. Premium volume, contribution and operating profit must appear as separate outputs.

For a simplified one-fee lead model, a useful diagnostic is:

**Maximum acquisition cost per acquired unit = qualified-request rate × paid acceptance rate × net lead fee × (1 − reversal fraction) − variable cost per acquired unit.**

This diagnostic assumes the stated fee and reversal basis and excludes fixed costs and cash timing. The monthly model should use the actual event and cost structure rather than treating this equation as a complete forecast.

Calculate revenue and contribution per acquired unit, paid yield, per-channel acquisition cost, payback, net revenue per payer, capacity and counterparty concentration. Customer value over the 36-month model window is a projection; call it validated lifetime value only when the evidence warrants that claim.

## Workbook structure

The future model should contain assumptions with provenance, cohort funnel, payable events, lead revenue, policy and renewal cohorts, service and software cohorts, cost drivers, monthly revenue and contribution, cash and receivables, scenarios and sensitivities, and validation checks. Keep native-currency detail beneath any consolidated view.

A supported baseline can be released only when critical payment and measurement inputs are substantiated. If they remain unavailable, release an explicitly uncalibrated scenario or symbolic model with the exact next evidence request.

# Uncertainty and validation

Create downside, base and upside cases from coherent assumptions. Avoid multiplying several optimistic upper bounds that depend on the same unproven change. Keep evidence confidence separate from commercial desirability.

Prioritise sensitivities for acquisition cost versus paid acceptance, net fee versus service cost, and renewal income versus continuing support burden. Also test a major payer or channel disappearing, slower payment, more reversals, missing data access and capacity constraints. Model frequent free use with zero additional remuneration as a direct test of the original retention concern.

Validation must check:

- Zero new acquisition or zero conversion creates no revenue from the affected new funnel; existing payable, renewal and receivable cohorts continue according to their terms.
- Absence of renewal rights produces no renewal revenue.
- Duplicate leads, cancelled policies and referrals are counted according to contract.
- Active-policy and subscriber balances reconcile by cohort and period.
- Cost scales with actual drivers, including free and failed work.
- Currency direction, aggregation and period definitions remain consistent.
- Revenue, invoices, cash, refunds and receivables reconcile without counting payments twice.
- Historical outcomes agree with available operational and finance evidence, with differences explained.

Keep observed months separate from forecast months. A holdout period is useful when enough history exists; otherwise disclose the limited calibration rather than creating an artificial validation claim. Formula and balance checks remain mandatory even when commercial data are sparse.

Allow a bounded repair loop of up to three attempts for identified model defects. If a material arithmetic or evidence defect remains, stop decision-ready release and preserve the defect and responsible next step.

# Gates and commercial decisions

| Gate | Pass condition | If the condition fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scope | Current category-country-channel status is explicit | Record unresolved scope |
| Commercial rights | Payable trigger and remuneration are supported | Continue symbolic/scenario work; defer calibrated base |
| Measurement | Denominators, attribution and costs reconcile | Flag affected inputs and confidence |
| Lane selection | At most two lanes selected against access and economics | Revisit selection |
| Fieldwork scope | External work is separately commissioned and authorised | Keep designs unexecuted |
| Fulfilment | Product, data and service access are established | Disable unsupported base offers |
| Model validity | Formulas, balances and provenance checks pass | Repair within the bound; block reliable release if unresolved |
| Decision | Proceed, revise, defer or stop has a reason and next evidence request | Preserve an unresolved decision |
| Ownership | Task outputs stay within their authorised owners | Block canonical promotion or broader effects |

A proceed decision needs a feasible delivery route and credible contribution under the chosen conservative case, a bounded acquisition or demand test, and a clear cash requirement. The operator must set acceptable payback, loss budget and service burden before a commercial experiment. These thresholds are business choices, not universal market benchmarks.

A revise decision applies when a specific price, channel or workflow change could alter the outcome. Defer when a critical term or access condition is unknown. Stop when the credible cases remain unattractive and no affordable next test can resolve that weakness.

Rank missing evidence by its likelihood of changing the decision and the cost of obtaining it. A confirmed net fee or rejection rule may be more valuable than another global sales report. Record what result would reverse the decision so the research remains falsifiable.

# Working sequence and deliverables

A planning sequence is four working weeks after the critical inputs are available: establish rights and measurement in week one; choose lanes and design demand/fulfilment work in week two; build and validate the model in week three; make the commercial decision and deliver the handoff in week four. This is a proposed sequence, not a delivery commitment. Waiting for terms and separately commissioned fieldwork can extend it; a 60–90 day pilot and annual renewal evidence have longer horizons.

The completed route should deliver the 36-month model, source and parameter registers, observed cohort and cost evidence, validation results, scenario sensitivities, a commercial decision memo and the next bounded research task. Each stage returns a traceable artifact and unresolved questions with an owner.

The strategy package contains a readable plan, an evidence-collection workbook, machine-readable dispatch, prioritised requests, blank parameter register, proposed experiment designs, baseline file identities and the Dispatch Spec validation result. The current validation confirms the route’s shape. It does not certify the eventual financial model or the business case.
